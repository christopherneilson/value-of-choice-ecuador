The Value of Choice in Centralized School Assignment · a toy market

Does lying pay?

Three families, three schools, one seat each. Rank the schools for your family and see where two assignment rules send you. Under the Boston mechanism a school fills its seat as soon as someone ranks it first, so a family that aims high and misses can lose its safe option too — and may do better by not telling the truth. Under deferred acceptance nothing is locked in until the end, and in the configuration the paper analyses no list does better than the truth. That is why the paper can treat Manta's lists as what families actually want.

      Under deferred acceptance the truthful list is among the best you can submit whatever the other families do — that is what "strategy-proof" means. The paper proves it for the configuration it analyses (static sibling priority, one quota per school, school-specific tie-breaking) and notes it is not immediate from the textbook result, because here listing a school is itself what earns you priority there. The deployed platform also offered dynamic sibling priority and family linking, which can break it; the paper's counterfactuals switch both off. Under Boston, whether lying pays depends on what everyone else does, which is exactly the guessing game a good mechanism removes.
